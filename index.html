Abstract

In naturalistic in-cabin driving datasets, the vast majority of clips show a near-stationary driver. We show this causes a systematic failure in all tested models trained with standard empirical risk minimisation (ERM): the optimizer under-weights the minority high-motion (HM) clips and over-predicts motion on the non-HM majority, making the trivial zero-velocity (ZV) baseline consistently better. We formalise this as the Velocity Dominance Score (VDS) and show this failure is structural: a provable consequence of ERM on class-imbalanced data. Empirically, VDS > 0.72 on every architecture we test, spanning a 193× parameter range and two independent datasets.

We further show the remedy is model- and dataset-agnostic: a lightweight regime gate (<5K parameters, 3 kinematic statistics → scalar routing score s) functions as a plug-in module that, when attached to each architecture we test, passes the VDS test. Applied to a 1M-parameter simple MLP, VDS drops 0.801→0.428 on AIDE and 0.781→0.458 on Drive&Act (3D OpenPose, 25 joints, different country), validating the remedy across datasets. Integrated into RaWM (Regime-aware World Model) built on Driver-WM, VDS drops 0.883→0.332 (95% CI [0.29, 0.37], N=609) with All-MPJPE improving by 28.6%. The gate also provides a real-time motion uncertainty signal applicable to proactive in-cabin safety systems without additional supervision.

Method / Architecture

RaWM adds three components to Driver-WM:

  1. Regime gate — 3 kinematic statistics → MLP → routing score s ∈ (0,1)
  2. ZV-anchored static branch — decodes the last observed latent, pulled toward ZV by a suppression loss
  3. HM loss reweighting — 5× upweight on high-motion clips in the rollout branch

Ŝ = (1−s)·Ŝstatic + s·Ŝrollout

Velocity Dominance Score (VDS)

VDS(M, D) = fraction of clips where ZV beats model M in per-clip MPJPE

A model passes the VDS test if VDS < 0.5, i.e. it beats ZV on the majority of clips. VDS > 0.72 held across every architecture we tested — a 193M-parameter VLM-conditioned model, a 1M-parameter MLP, and a 200K-parameter LSTM — on two datasets (AIDE, Drive&Act). Every architecture with the regime gate attached passes the VDS test.
